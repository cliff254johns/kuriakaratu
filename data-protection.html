Data Protection & Your Rights

Effective date: 15 November 2026 · Last reviewed: 15 November 2026

This notice supplements the Privacy Policy and explains how to exercise data-protection rights in relation to personal data handled by Kuria Karatu & Company Advocates. The principal Kenyan framework includes the Data Protection Act, 2019 and related regulations.

1. Data controller / processor roles

The firm may act as a data controller when it determines the purposes and means of processing its own business, website, enquiry, personnel or client-administration data. In some client or outsourced arrangements, the firm may act as a data processor on documented instructions. The role must be assessed for each processing activity rather than assumed from the relationship label.

2. Data protection principles

Personal data should be processed lawfully, fairly and transparently; collected for specified legitimate purposes; limited to what is necessary; kept accurate; retained no longer than necessary; and protected through appropriate safeguards. Transfers outside Kenya require a lawful basis and applicable safeguards.

3. Your rights

Subject to the Act, regulations and applicable exemptions, a data subject may request information about processing; access personal data; correction of false or misleading data; deletion of false or misleading data; object to processing; and exercise other rights available under the Act, including rights concerning consent and portability where applicable.

4. How to make a request

  1. Email info@kuriakaratuadvocates.com with the subject “Data Protection Request”.
  2. Explain the right you wish to exercise, the relevant interaction or matter, and the outcome you seek.
  3. We may request proportionate information to verify your identity or authority to act for another person. Do not email identity documents unless we first provide an appropriate secure method.
  4. We will assess and respond within the time required by applicable law. Some requests may be limited by legal privilege, court orders, professional obligations, legal claims, or other lawful exemptions.

5. Complaints

If you are dissatisfied with our response, you may contact the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) through its complaints process. The ODPC publishes information about data-subject rights.

6. Personal data breaches

The firm maintains responsibility for assessing and managing suspected personal-data breaches in accordance with applicable law, including any required notifications to the ODPC and affected individuals. A processor engaged by the firm should notify the firm promptly under the relevant written agreement. This public notice is not a breach-reporting channel.
